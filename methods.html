Data extraction and curation methods

StimBank is an open database of human intracranial stimulation responses. Each entry in the database represents a unique stimulation event with its corresponding stimulation site coordinates and evoked response. These data were extracted and curated through systematic review of the literature. The full methodological details will be reported in a scientific publication, but a summary is presented below.

Systematic Review

The full methodological details will be reported in a scientific publication, but a summary is presented below. In brief, we conducted a systematic review according to the PRISMA guidelines to identify studies that reported functional responses to acute intracranial stimulation in humans and included information on the corresponding stimulation site locations. This typically included functional responses occurring during or immediately after brief stimulation epochs lasting seconds to minutes. Because a large proportion of studies involved patients with epilepsy undergoing seizure monitoring or stimulation mapping, stimulations that triggered seizures or were explicitly described as performed within the seizure onset zone were excluded. Articles lacking stereotactic coordinates, precise images, or detailed schematics allowing localization of the stimulation sites were excluded. We considered all articles published until September 11th 2025. 7,924 articles were screened (see our PubMed search here), and a total of 269 were deemed eligible for inclusion in the final database.

Data Extraction

The following data were extracted for each publication and stimulation event:

  • Study characteristics: first author, year of publication, country, overall cohort sample size, and number of patients reported.
  • Patient characteristics: handedness, sex, age, underlying disease, and type of EBS procedure.
  • Stimulation parameters: frequency, amplitude or voltage, pulse width, and duration.
  • Stimulation location: hemisphere, brain region, and MNI coordinates.
  • Reported functional response.

MNI coordinates of all stimulation sites were obtained as follows:

  • MNI coordinates were directly extracted, if available.
  • Talairach coordinates were converted to MNI space using Ginger ALE (version 3.0.2).
  • AC-PC coordinates were converted to MNI space using the dedicated tool in the Lead-DBS software.
  • For studies where coordinates were not provided, stimulation sites were manually mapped to the MNI template (MNI152 non-linear asymmetric 2009b) using 3D Slicer based on the available figures. This included MRI slices, 3-D surface reconstructions, craniotomy pictures, and/or precise schematics.
  • To ensure reliability, all mappings were performed by one reviewer (G.M.M., Z.D.) and independently verified by the other.

All stimulation coordinates were cross-checked against the anatomical labels reported by the original authors by a third reviewer (A.E.) to ensure consistency. Given these variable data sources, the precision of localization may vary across entries. Despite this limitation, we believe that StimBank provides a valuable resource for investigating brain function, especially when aggregating stimulation sites across studies. Our procedure for manual mapping of stimulation sites is analogous to manual tracing of lesions from figures, which is widely used in the lesion network mapping literature and has been shown to produce network mapping results consistent with those derived from lesions directly segmented on patient imaging (see Cohen et al., 2019; Ferguson et al., 2019). Nevertheless, users should still consider potential localization uncertainty when interpreting the data or conducting analyses that depend on precise anatomical localization.

Classification of stimulation-evoked functional responses

To the best of our knowledge, there is currently no established classification scheme for EBS responses. We developed a hierarchical classification scheme adapted from the ILAE seizure semiological feature classification, with refinements to better capture the diversity of functional responses evoked by intracranial stimulation. Of note, domains and sub-domains are not mutually exclusive, and a single response could be classified under multiple domains or sub-domains. Rather than claiming to provide a definitive ontology of functional responses to stimulation, we focused on providing clear definitions of categories and promoting reliability and consistency in classification through independent review, with disagreements being resolved by consensus between the two reviewers. We also retained the response descriptions reported in the original publications, allowing users to reclassify responses according to their own research questions or alternative classification schemes.

AI Statement

Data extraction and curation was exclusively performed by human researchers, without reliance on AI technologies. To promote data accuracy and consistency, the most critical steps (i.e., response classification and stimulation site mappings) were verified by a second reviewer, and any disagreement or uncertainties was resolved through discussion.

Data characteristics overview

  • Study characteristics (N, country of origin, date of publication)
  • Patient characteristics (N, disease, age, sex, handedness)
  • Stimulation procedure and parameters (EBS type, frequency, PW, intensity)
  • Stimulation location (hemisphere, lobe) and type of coordinates (MNI / ACPC / Talairach / mapped)
  • Functional responses (N per domain)
